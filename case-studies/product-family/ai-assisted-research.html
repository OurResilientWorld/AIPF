AI-Assisted Research: sharing a method without standardising the conclusions

Testing which research controls transfer across subjects—and where a shared approach needs to change.

By Rick Molony · developed with AI assistance

Cross-product learning · bounded pattern tests

AI-Assisted Research examines whether related research products can share useful controls without carrying weak assumptions from one subject to another. Each product keeps its own question, audience and conclusions.

The family shares ways to define scope, identify evidence, challenge claims and return learning. It does not require every subject to use the same report structure or reach the same answer.

Why a family view helps

A change to a shared template or review rule can affect several research products. Those shared choices need an owner. At the same time, a product owner must be able to adapt or decline an approach that does not fit the subject.

The family therefore treats a reusable pattern as a proposal with a stated context and evidence boundary, rather than as a compulsory template.

The pattern under test

The hypothesis-led research pattern starts with a named audience, explicit questions, accessible sources and available review. Work then tests the hypotheses, checks individual reports and the relationships between reports, and derives summaries without adding new claims.

Some controls should remain consistent: scope traceability, contrary evidence, checking and human acceptance. Other choices can vary: subject, report count, analytical method and how one report builds on another. A trilogy is not required when one report answers the question.

Sharing also has a cost. A common control can consume review time or distribute a weak assumption. When a handoff or scope check fails, the affected output is held, corrected and checked again.

These are development and operator observations. They are not representative reader research or measured delivery savings.

A related product tests a different kind of reuse

The Commonwealth Budget Explorer is a simulation, not another hypothesis-led report. Its experience exposed a related family question: does a reusable approach carry the purpose of each section, or only its visible features?

The first private version had working controls and calculations but did not explain enough about the starting position. Comparison with a reference explorer led to clearer baseline context, connected evidence, richer history and a more useful first experiment.

The improved explorer was accepted on 24 October 2026 and published separately on the research site on 25 October. Those decisions do not validate the hypothesis-research pattern or merge it with the simulation pattern. The Product case study explains the individual product work.

What changed in the reusable approach

The learning is to map the reader’s task before transferring an interface. Explain the baseline, distinguish levels from changes, connect evidence to editable assumptions, and test returning from detail without losing the scenario.

Record arithmetic checks, owner usefulness review and reader comprehension separately. When a shared generator or template changes, verify that a newly prepared product actually receives the revised material. Existing products must not silently inherit new domain assumptions.

These changes concern both experience and shared implementation. They do not establish that a particular defect in a generator caused the original explanatory gap.

The family decision remains bounded

The earlier family record allowed limited testing and exposed useful failures. Its broader reuse and measured-benefit decision remains unrecorded in the evidence used for this article. Acceptance of individual outputs is not a family-wide Scale decision.

The next useful evidence would come from an intended reader explaining a product’s baseline, making and interpreting a change, finding its supporting evidence and recovering without coaching. No general reader benefit or efficiency gain is claimed here.

What guide readers can take away

Use the Product Family Guide to decide what should be shared, who owns it and how products return evidence. Keep product-specific acceptance. Record adaptations and non-use as legitimate outcomes. Recheck affected products when a shared source, model or control changes.

The aim is a dependable shared approach that earns its place through evidence, rather than uniform outputs.
