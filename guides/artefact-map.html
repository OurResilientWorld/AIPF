Artefact Map

Keep the evidence that helps you decide, share and improve AI work.

By Rick Molony and Ghulam Akbar · developed with AI assistance

On this page

  • Product: eight records
  • Family: five records when products share a decision
  • Portfolio: five records for investment choices
  • Keep the records useful
  • Use the records in a portfolio review

These artefacts help you see what you know, what remains uncertain and who can decide what happens next. They connect tests to decisions, make costs and responsibilities visible, and help teams reuse evidence without losing its limits. Keep only the detail needed for the next decision.

Start with one decision

An artefact, or record, can be a note, prototype, test or view in an existing tool. Start with a real question, name who can decide, and use the relevant records below:

  • Product: understand and test one product or workflow.
  • Family: coordinate a shared outcome, dependency or reuse decision across products.
  • Portfolio: decide how to commit money, people and oversight across AI work.

The guides name eighteen core records and an optional Pattern. They need not be separate documents. Use the linked definitions for detail; this map adds no new roles or approvals.

Product: eight records

Use these to understand the need, test an approach and decide its permitted use. Agree maintainers using the Product responsibilities.

Record What to keep
Product Intent People and need, expected outcome, boundaries and signs of success.
Assumption and Evidence Map Claims, sources, uncertainty and contrary findings; a result that could defeat the idea and a useful test.
Concept Artefact A sketch, example or simulation of the intended experience, shown before building.
Roadmap Options Credible alternatives, including process change or waiting, with cost, dependencies, checking needs and the next question.
AI Model Harness Permitted AI actions, approved sources, tested checks, reviewers, logging, recovery and what must stop or return to the owner. Recheck affected controls after changes.
End-to-End Evidence Slice A narrow connected test: version, question, cases, criteria, results, failures, recovery and limits. Label simulated parts.
Acceptance Record Accepted version and use, accepter and authority, evidence, limits, conditions, support and next review.
Learning Record A finding, the decision or practice it changes, the change owner and affected work.

Family: five records when products share a decision

Each product keeps its own acceptance responsibilities. The Family Guide names record owners; a family may test its own shared boundary or reuse claim.

Record What to keep
Dependency and Integration Map Family outline: outcome, products, people and open questions; handoffs, owners, source versions, permissions, checks and failure recovery.
Shared Substrate Map Common tools, information, models and controls; owners, supported uses, capacity, costs and recovery.
Coordination Function Map Who supplies, receives and judges work; waiting decisions, available reviewers, work limits and escalation. Separate AI routing from human decisions.
Cross-Product Evidence Ledger Links to original evidence, tested contexts, versions, methods, contrary findings and permitted reuse.
Family Benefits Map Expected and observed benefit by product, measure, baseline, period, source, shared costs and extra work or harm. Count shared outcomes once.
Pattern — optional A reusable approach: problem, suitable contexts, variations, trade-offs, evidence, permitted uses and review or retirement conditions. Adoption is not automatic.

Portfolio: five records for investment choices

A bet is a bounded commitment to answer a question. Use the Portfolio responsibilities to assign maintenance and judgement.

Record What to keep
AI Bet Ledger Purpose, spending owner, evidence, decision, next request and stop/review conditions. Separate actual spending, commitments and forecasts.
Decision-Capacity Ledger Review and support demand, skills, available time and work limits. Distinguish waiting for evidence from waiting for judgement.
Model and Vendor Concentration Register Shared suppliers, models, sources and checking arrangements, affected bets, owners and response options.
Benefits Realisation Map Promised versus observed benefits, owners, measures, baselines, periods, sources, costs, extra work or harm. Link product/family results; avoid double counting.
Autonomy Boundary Map Permitted AI actions, permission owners, required checks and human intervention. Link tested controls for the actual task.

Keep the records useful

  • Link to one source. Give important evidence and decisions an identifier, owner, date/version, source and limits. Product and Family retain original findings; Portfolio uses them to reconsider investment. Reuse links rather than copy facts.
  • Make uncertainty visible. Distinguish observations, estimates and AI-generated material. Mark missing information unknown, not yet observed, not supplied or not applicable, with a reason. None of these states means zero or approval.
  • Name the authority. A maintainer does not gain approval rights. The Product owner, Family owner or Portfolio owner (or its board) holds the relevant gate decision—the decision about the next commitment. Spending and AI permissions retain their authorised owners. The maker cannot be the sole accepter.
  • Separate decision states. Progress, evidence, recommendation, funding commitment, release, acceptance and permission to scale are different. Record exactly what was authorised. Scale is available only at Gate 3, the last of three phase gates, when evidence and readiness support it; see the Framework.
  • Update when learning changes the work. Preserve earlier versions, identify affected tests and decisions, and ask their owners to reassess. Pause affected use when agreed risk rules require it.

Use the Framework’s shared information checklist and phase handoffs for more detail. Check AI controls for each intended task; a permission map or a successful test elsewhere does not establish that they work here.

Use the records in a portfolio review

Use this cycle when records feed a portfolio decision. A routine review is not automatically a phase gate. A product can connect directly to Portfolio when no Family decision is needed.

For a worked example, follow BET-017 in the Implementation Guide. Its fictional result leads to Revise, with benefit still uncertain. Use your own decision to check that another reader can find the evidence, owner, conditions and next allowed action.
